6-DOF Robotic Arm

Electrical power requirements, current budgeting, and battery-to-power-distribution wiring for a six-degree-of-freedom robotic arm being used in a haptic teleoperation project.

01 / Project Overview

Electrical engineering behind a moving system

This work supports an existing 6-DOF robotic arm subsystem within a wider haptic teleoperation project. My contribution focuses on evaluating electrical power needs and the practical wiring constraints that connect battery power to the rest of the system.

The goal of the analysis is to inform component and cable selection, with attention to continuous load, possible current peaks, conductor length, and allowable voltage drop.

My contribution: electrical analysis and evaluation of the power path. This page describes design calculations and selection considerations, not a claim that the full robotic-arm electrical system has been manufactured or validated by me.
02 / Electrical Contributions

What I worked on

01 · POWER DEMAND

Motor & electronics load assessment

Analyzed motor and electronic subsystem requirements to estimate current demand and identify the electrical loads relevant to the robot's power distribution design.

02 · DISTRIBUTION

Battery-to-distribution wiring

Evaluated the wiring path between the battery and power distribution board, considering the implications of cable run length and load current.

03 · CABLE SELECTION

Wire gauge & current capacity

Compared copper wire gauge options against continuous-current requirements and considered transient or peak-current conditions when assessing suitability.

04 · VOLTAGE LOSS

Voltage-drop considerations

Examined how conductor resistance and round-trip cable length influence voltage drop, informing trade-offs among cable size, flexibility, and electrical performance.

04 / Analysis Method

How cable selection was evaluated

1. Estimate load current

Account for the continuous operating demand of the relevant subsystems and separately review higher-load conditions.

2. Check conductor suitability

Compare candidate cable sizes using the installation conditions and the appropriate current-carrying limits.

3. Estimate voltage drop

Delta V = I × Rloop

Use total circuit resistance, including the outgoing and return conductors, rather than only one-way cable length.

4. Review trade-offs

Consider physical cable flexibility and routing alongside electrical losses and possible peak-current constraints.

Engineering focus: translating load requirements into practical wiring decisions, with attention to safe current handling, voltage availability at the load, and design trade-offs.
